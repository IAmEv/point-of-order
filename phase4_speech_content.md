
# Phase 4 Speech Content (v2)

Full speech text for the 2-for/2-against debate stage, keyed by topic bucket and by which bloc authored the paper under debate. Each paper has exactly 3 possible speakers per side (Power, Dem-Boy, Carolina of the relevant bloc, minus slacker); the player picks 2 of the 3 for each side, so all 3 must be written for each side/paper combination.

**Role rules (apply regardless of for/against, except Power-Del, see below):**
- **Carolina-style del**: jokes about the title of whichever paper is currently on the floor, referencing the specific idea their bloc raised in caucus.
- **Dem-Boy from the power-led bloc**: glazes their boss ({boss}) by name.
- **Dem-Boy from the carolina-led bloc**: speaks with new confidence/empowerment, championing the idea they personally raised in caucus.

**Power-Del rules (v2, tone locked):**
- **Speaking AGAINST any paper** (regardless of which bloc they belong to): always cutthroat and ruthless, tearing the paper apart with confidence and proper grammar. No sloppiness here, this is their strongest register.
- **Speaking FOR the paper of the bloc they led** (i.e. the power-led bloc's own paper): clean, dominant, confidently claims every idea in the paper as their own vision, wins by asserting authority rather than debating specifics.
- **Speaking FOR the paper of the bloc they did NOT lead** (i.e. this Power-Del sits in the carolina-led bloc, speaking for that bloc's own paper): still speaks with confidence and proper grammar, tries to claim credit for pieces of the idea, but cannot go in depth on it and quickly pivots to a subjective, feelings-based reason the paper is simply the best one on the floor.

Placeholders: `{country}` = speaker's own country. `{boss}` = the power-led bloc's Power-Del's country. `{carolinaLeadCountry}` = the carolina-led bloc's Carolina-style del's country. `{paperName}` = the paper currently on the floor's title.

## SUBSTANCE
Power-led paper: **Sanctions Enforcement Framework** (flat 2% GDP contribution, no exceptions)
Carolina-led paper: **Fund Me Maybe** (sliding scale by GDP, floor of 0.5%)

### Paper: Sanctions Enforcement Framework (power-authored)

**FOR pool (power-led bloc):**
- *Power (leading, FOR):* "This framework exists because {country} built it, start to finish. Two percent of GDP, every country, no exceptions. I heard the idea of carve-outs for smaller economies floated in our own caucus and I killed it there for a reason. This room does not need a debate. It needs a number, and I already gave you the right one."
- *Dem-Boy (glazes, FOR):* "{boss} had this figured out from the start. Everyone pays the same two percent, no loopholes, no excuses. That is what real leadership on this issue looks like. This paper deserves your support."
- *Carolina (jokes, FOR):* "Look, I'll be honest, when I heard 'Sanctions Enforcement Framework' I thought we were naming a law firm. I actually pitched softer rates for smaller economies in our own room and got shut down flat. But under the boring name is a real idea, and better a strict paper than no paper."

**AGAINST pool (carolina-led bloc):**
- *Power (against, ruthless):* "Let's be direct. A flat two percent is lazy math dressed up as fairness. It does not account for a single real economy in this room, and it collapses the moment one country simply cannot pay it and walks. My bloc's floor system solves the exact problem this paper pretends does not exist. Vote it down."
- *Dem-Boy (empowered, AGAINST):* "We looked at this closely, and a flat two percent does not account for the fact that not every country can absorb that cost the same way. Our bloc's sliding scale gets to the same funding goal without breaking smaller delegations. This paper isn't there yet."
- *Carolina (jokes, AGAINST):* "'Sanctions Enforcement Framework,' very official, very scary. But a scary name doesn't fix the fact that flat rates hit small countries the hardest. We came up with a sliding scale for a reason. Vote this one down."

### Paper: Fund Me Maybe (carolina-authored)

**FOR pool (carolina-led bloc):**
- *Carolina (jokes, FOR):* "Yes, it's called Fund Me Maybe, and no, I'm not sorry. But behind the name is the actual fix: a sliding scale, so rich countries pay more, poor countries pay less, and nobody gets crushed by a flat number. Funny name, serious paper."
- *Dem-Boy (empowered, FOR):* "This is the paper {country} actually helped build from scratch. A sliding scale by GDP, with a real floor so no country pays less than half a percent. It gets to the same funding total, fairly. That is worth backing."
- *Power (non-leading, FOR, sloppy/subjective):* "I'll point out that the floor in this paper, half a percent minimum, that piece came directly out of my own mouth in caucus. As for the sliding math itself, the exact brackets, I'll leave that to the room to review. What I'll say is it simply feels like the fairer paper, and fair is what gets votes in here. Support it."

**AGAINST pool (power-led bloc):**
- *Power (leading, AGAINST, ruthless):* "A sliding scale sounds compassionate right up until someone has to calculate it. My paper already solved this. Two percent, flat, for everyone, no argument, no bracket to dispute, no country pretending their economy is smaller than it is. This paper is a spreadsheet problem wearing a fairness costume. Reject it."
- *Dem-Boy (glazes, AGAINST):* "{boss} said it best, exceptions and brackets are exactly how enforcement falls apart. A sliding scale is just exceptions with extra steps. We should stick with the flat rate."
- *Carolina (jokes, AGAINST):* "I like a good sliding scale as much as the next delegate, but 'Fund Me Maybe' might be a little too honest about how uncertain this funding actually ends up being. A flat number, at least, is a number."

## PROCESS
Power-led paper: **Next Steps Protocol** (6-month start, 1-year full implementation, no check-ins)
Carolina-led paper: **Define and Conquer** (phased rollout, simple parts first, then harder parts)

### Paper: Next Steps Protocol (power-authored)

**FOR pool (power-led bloc):**
- *Power (leading, FOR):* "I set this timeline myself. Six months to start, one year to finish, the same clock for every country in this room. Someone in my own caucus wanted a check-in built in halfway through. I refused, because a check-in is just a scheduled excuse to fall behind. This paper works because I did not let it get soft."
- *Dem-Boy (glazes, FOR):* "{boss} was right that check-ins just turn into extensions. One clear timeline keeps everyone moving instead of stalling. This paper gets it done."
- *Carolina (jokes, FOR):* "'Next Steps Protocol,' very serious, very committee-brochure. I pushed for a full year to start in our own room and got told no on the spot. Still, I'll give it this, at least nobody can pretend they didn't know the deadline."

**AGAINST pool (carolina-led bloc):**
- *Power (against, ruthless):* "One deadline for every country in this room is not discipline, it is indifference. It assumes every delegation starts from the same place, and none of them do. My bloc built a version with actual phases, because a timeline that ignores capacity is not a plan, it is a countdown clock nobody can survive. Vote no."
- *Dem-Boy (empowered, AGAINST):* "A single deadline for every country ignores that some delegations need to build things up in stages. Our version does the easy parts first so people can see progress before the hard part even starts. That is a stronger plan."
- *Carolina (jokes, AGAINST):* "Six months to start, one year to finish, no check-ins in between, that's less a protocol and more a countdown clock with no pit stops. We built in phases for a reason. Vote no."

### Paper: Define and Conquer (carolina-authored)

**FOR pool (carolina-led bloc):**
- *Carolina (jokes, FOR):* "Define and Conquer, because we're doing this in order instead of all at once. Simple stuff first, so people can actually see this working before we ask them to commit to the hard parts. That's not slow, that's smart."
- *Dem-Boy (empowered, FOR):* "{country} helped build the actual phase structure here. Basics first, trust built early, harder pieces once people believe in it. That gets more countries to actually finish, not just start."
- *Power (non-leading, FOR, sloppy/subjective):* "The one-year cap on the whole process, keeping a hard end date even with the phases built in, that part came from me. I won't walk through every phase boundary line by line, that's not necessary tonight. What matters is this paper actually respects how people work, and that's the kind of paper that earns support in this room."

**AGAINST pool (power-led bloc):**
- *Power (leading, AGAINST, ruthless):* "Phases sound thoughtful and function as delay. Every phase boundary in this paper is another place for a country to quietly fall behind and blame the schedule. My timeline gives you one deadline and nowhere to hide. That is not harsh, that is honest. Reject this."
- *Dem-Boy (glazes, AGAINST):* "{boss} called it, phases and check-ins are just extensions with a different name. A single clock keeps everybody honest."
- *Carolina (jokes, AGAINST):* "I like the idea of Define and Conquer, but 'define' can take a very long time in this room. Sometimes a flat deadline is what actually gets a paper conquered."

## PEOPLE
Power-led paper: **Humanitarian Relief Framework** (pre-written plan, no outside consultation, no root-cause language)
Carolina-led paper: **Heart of the Matter** (consults affected groups, addresses root causes)

### Paper: Humanitarian Relief Framework (power-authored)

**FOR pool (power-led bloc):**
- *Power (leading, FOR):* "I wrote this plan myself, and I stand behind every line of it. Someone in my own caucus wanted us to go out and consult the people it affects before we finalized anything. We do not need to. I already accounted for what they need. This plan is complete, and it is ready to vote on tonight."
- *Dem-Boy (glazes, FOR):* "Right, {boss} and I did the research. We do not need to redo it."
- *Carolina (jokes, FOR):* "'Humanitarian Relief Framework,' about as warm a title as a tax form. I wanted a line in here saying we actually talked to the people affected, and got told no because we hadn't. At least it's an honest paper, even if it's a cold one."

**AGAINST pool (carolina-led bloc):**
- *Power (against, ruthless):* "A relief plan written entirely in a room, by people who never once spoke to anyone it claims to help, is not a plan, it is a guess with a cover page. My bloc actually went and asked what people needed instead of assuming it. Reject a paper built on assumptions."
- *Dem-Boy (empowered, AGAINST):* "If we just respond without ever fixing what caused this, we are back here in five years dealing with the exact same crisis. Our paper actually goes after the cause. This one refuses to even mention it."
- *Carolina (jokes, AGAINST):* "This paper is called Humanitarian Relief Framework and doesn't ask a single affected person what they think. Our paper actually goes to the heart of the matter. Vote for the one that listens."

### Paper: Heart of the Matter (carolina-authored)

**FOR pool (carolina-led bloc):**
- *Carolina (jokes, FOR):* "Heart of the Matter, because we actually went looking for one, unlike some papers on this floor. We talked to the people this affects and we're not afraid to name why this keeps happening. That's not soft, that's the whole point."
- *Dem-Boy (empowered, FOR):* "If we just help people now but never fix the actual cause, we're back here in five years having this same debate. This paper finally goes after why this keeps happening, not just what to do about it today."
- *Power (non-leading, FOR, sloppy/subjective):* "I negotiated the root-cause section down to two sentences in our own caucus, so I can tell you it's there and it's tight, I won't pretend I can walk you through every line of the reasoning behind it right now. What I'll say is this paper simply feels more honest than a plan someone wrote alone at a desk, and honesty is worth a vote."

**AGAINST pool (power-led bloc):**
- *Power (leading, AGAINST, ruthless):* "Root causes make for a nice speech and a useless deadline. My plan is already written, already complete, and already accounts for what is needed without turning this into an open-ended listening tour. This paper trades results for feelings. Reject it."
- *Dem-Boy (glazes, AGAINST):* "{boss} already thought through every angle on this. We don't need to reopen a plan that's already finished."
- *Carolina (jokes, AGAINST):* "I appreciate a paper with heart, truly, but sometimes a room needs a plan more than it needs a feeling. The other paper is already written and ready to go."

## OPENING
Power-led paper: **Joint Position Statement** (leads with Power's own point, one clear message)
Carolina-led paper: **Position Yourself** (opens with root-cause context/story, then the ask)

### Paper: Joint Position Statement (power-authored)

**FOR pool (power-led bloc):**
- *Power (leading, FOR):* "When we speak, we lead with my point, because it is the strongest thing in this room and I am not interested in diluting it. Someone in my own caucus wanted to open by naming every country's contribution instead. I said no. One message, delivered clearly, wins more than a list of names ever will."
- *Dem-Boy (glazes, FOR):* "Right, one message hits harder than five. {boss}'s point goes first, and everything else follows."
- *Carolina (jokes, FOR):* "'Joint Position Statement,' about as exciting as it sounds. I wanted us to open by naming everyone who worked on this and got told that wastes time. It's blunt, but at least the room knows exactly what we're saying and who's saying it."

**AGAINST pool (carolina-led bloc):**
- *Power (against, ruthless):* "One person's point, delivered first and loudest, is not a strategy, it is an ego with a microphone. My bloc opens with why this actually matters to the people in this room, and that earns real support instead of just attention. Reject the paper that leads with a name instead of a reason."
- *Dem-Boy (empowered, AGAINST):* "Everyone already knows what the problem is on paper. What they don't know is why it matters to them. Our opening actually makes people care before we ask for anything. This paper skips straight to the ask."
- *Carolina (jokes, AGAINST):* "Nothing says 'trust us' like an opening statement that's really just one delegate's highlight reel. We open with the actual story. Vote for the paper that starts with a reason to care."

### Paper: Position Yourself (carolina-authored)

**FOR pool (carolina-led bloc):**
- *Carolina (jokes, FOR):* "Position Yourself, because before you ask a room for anything, you have to give them a reason to care. We open with the real story behind this, not just a name and a demand. Yes, the title's a little on the nose. It's still the right move."
- *Dem-Boy (empowered, FOR):* "If we open with the cause, with why this actually matters, people care before we ever get to the ask. That's a stronger opening than jumping straight to a demand. This paper gets that."
- *Power (non-leading, FOR, sloppy/subjective):* "I'm the one who insisted the opening story stay short so the actual point still lands fast, so I can tell you that compromise is built in. I won't walk through the full story beat by beat right now. What matters is this opening simply connects better with a room than jumping straight to a demand does, and that's worth voting for."

**AGAINST pool (power-led bloc):**
- *Power (leading, AGAINST, ruthless):* "An opening built around a story is a stall tactic wearing empathy as a costume. My statement leads with the point because the point is what moves a room, not a warm-up act before it. This paper wastes the only moment you have everyone's attention. Reject it."
- *Dem-Boy (glazes, AGAINST):* "{boss}'s point is the clearest thing we have. It should go first, not get buried under a story."
- *Carolina (jokes, AGAINST):* "A good story is nice, but by the time this paper gets to the actual ask, half the room's already checked their phone. Sometimes you just lead with the point."
